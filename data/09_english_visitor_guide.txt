Prachinburi Province: A Short Visitor Guide (English)

Prachinburi is a province in eastern Thailand, within the Bang Pakong river basin, and the Prachinburi River flows through it. It is a convenient weekend destination from Bangkok and combines forest, adventure activities and ancient history.

Thap Lan National Park is the second largest national park in Thailand, covering about 1,397,375 rai. It contains the last large palm forest (lan forest) in the country and Pha Kep Tawan, a popular viewpoint for sunrise and sunset.

Pang Sida National Park, shared with Sa Kaeo province, is known as the land of butterflies in the eastern forest. Together with Thap Lan it belongs to the Dong Phayayen-Khao Yai Forest Complex, a UNESCO natural World Heritage Site inscribed in 2005.

White-water rafting takes place at Kaeng Hin Phoeng in Kabin Buri district on the Prachinburi River. The route passes seven rapids. A trip lasts roughly 30 minutes to 1.5 hours, the best months are July to November, and packages start at around 750 baht per person.

Si Mahosot Ancient City dates from the Dvaravati period (about the 11th to 16th Buddhist centuries). Highlights include the pair of Buddha footprints carved in laterite, about 3 metres in size, and Sa Morakot, where the remains of an ancient hospital (arogyasala) were found.

Travel tips: a private car is the most convenient way to get around because attractions are far apart. Check weather and park announcements before travelling.
